## Final sentence (114 characters)

> The escalator handrail moved faster than the steps, so my hand reached the top a moment before the rest of me did.

## Reasoning against the 5 judge criteria

1. **Small moment of wonder in one sentence** — riding an escalator is about
   as everyday as it gets; the whole moment is a single ride, a single
   sentence.
2. **Angle the reader has not met before** — the surprise is not the ride
   itself but the handrail outrunning the steps: the hand arrives before its
   owner. Nobody writes about the handrail; the phrasing itself carries the
   jolt, and the body is quietly split in two ("my hand ... the rest of me").
3. **Lands in one read** — one clause sets the fact, the second clause pays
   it off immediately; no subordinate detour, no second image to untangle.
4. **Grounded in something real and specific** — escalator handrails really
   do run slightly faster than the steps (a known, verifiable quirk of the
   machine), so the strangeness is anchored in a concrete mechanism, not a
   floating metaphor.
5. **Every word working** — no adjectives for decoration, no worn phrase;
   "handrail", "steps", "hand", "the rest of me" are all load-bearing.

## Rejected alternatives

- *The vending machine dropped two chocolate bars and kept the change...*
  — "generous machine" is a familiar joke shape; the surprise is explained
  rather than felt.
- *My receipt listed the milk twice, as if the till had sold me the same
  morning twice* — decent, but "sold me the same morning twice" leans on a
  time-loop trope the judge has likely met.
- *The dryer stopped and every sock had paired off with a stranger's* —
  playful, but the lost-sock trope is worn; the pairing idea is one step
  from a joke everyone has heard.
- *The crossing light turned green for exactly no one, and the street
  obeyed anyway* — too abstract; the wonder floats free of a felt moment.
- Anything with clocks, a warm bus-stop bench, an empty floor, a lift door
  opening on a stranger's song, a kettle about to click, or a puddle
  holding the sky — all explicitly avoided (description's own examples and
  the early field's tropes).
